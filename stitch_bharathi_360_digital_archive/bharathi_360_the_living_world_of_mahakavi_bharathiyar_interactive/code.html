1910 Sanctum

Mahakavi's Study in Pondicherry

"Surrounded by police spies and British surveillance just across the border, Bharathi’s writing desk in French Pondicherry was lit by a single kerosene brass lamp."

On this modest wooden table between 1910 and 1918, Bharathiyar translated the Bhagavad Gita into simple Tamil, authored Kuyil Pattu, penned the revolutionary chapters of Panchali Sabatham, and hosted patriot Sri Aurobindo.

Desk Artifacts Brass inkpot, peacock quill, reed pen, handwritten India magazine drafts.
Atmosphere Bay of Bengal coastal breeze, chants of Vedic hymns, freedom pamphlets.

Complete Chronology

Mahakavi Bharathiyar Complete Timeline

1882 — Birth

Born on Dec 11 in Ettayapuram to Chinnaswamy Subramania Iyer and Lakshmi Ammal.

1893 — Title of Bharathi

Honored by the Ettayapuram court for exceptional poetic mastery at age 11.

1904 — Journalism in Madurai & Chennai

Serves as Tamil teacher in Sethupathi High School and later joins Swadesamitran.

1906 — Nationalist Awakening

Attends Surat Congress, meets Tilak, edits the fiery 'India' newspaper.

1910 — Pondicherry Exile

Moves to French Pondicherry to escape British arrest warrants; prolific literary output.

1918 — Return to British India

Crosses back to British territory at Cuddalore; briefly imprisoned.

1921 — Passing

Passes away on September 11, 1921 in Triplicane, Chennai after being blessed by Parthasarathy temple elephant.

Bharathiyar's journey spanned from the southern plains of Ettayapuram and Kadayam, to the temple city of Madurai, the publishing capital of Chennai, and the revolutionary sanctuary of French Pondicherry. Each location birthed a distinct dimension of his genius.

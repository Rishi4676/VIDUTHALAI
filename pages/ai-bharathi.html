"What was Bharathiyar's core philosophy regarding women's liberation, education, and social sovereignty?"

1.0 CORE PHILOSOPHICAL THESIS Universal Parity through Shakti (பெண்மை வாழ்கென்று கூத்திடுவோமடா)

Subramania Bharati rejected reformist tokenism in favor of absolute spiritual and socioeconomic sovereignty for women. Grounded in the non-dual Vedantic theology of Parashakti, he contended that an enslaved womanhood inevitably renders an entire civilization sterile and incapable of true political swaraj (freedom).

“ஏட்டையும் பtraikkum பெண்கள் தொடுவது தீமையென் றெண்ணி யிருந்தவர் மாய்ந்து விட்டார்; வீட்டுக்குள்ளே பெண்ணைப் பூட்டிவைப்போ மென்ற விந்தை மனிதர் தலைகவிழ்ந்தார்.”

— கவிதை: ‘பெண்கள் விடுதலைக் கும்மி’ (1908)

To Bharati, women’s freedom was not an auxiliary goal post-independence; it was the prerequisite engine of Indian renaissance. He maintained that denying literacy and self-determination to women while seeking freedom from British rule was a moral contradiction.

3.0 CRITICAL HISTORICAL CONTEXT The 1905 Calcutta Encounter with Sister Nivedita

During his journey to the Banaras Indian National Congress session in 1905, Bharati met Sister Nivedita (Margaret Noble), disciple of Swami Vivekananda, in Calcutta. When Bharati admitted he had left his wife Chellamma at home due to social orthodoxy, Nivedita sharply rebuked him:

“How can you deliver the motherland if you treat your own companion as a subservient dependent? Liberation begins at your hearth.”

Bharati experienced an existential transformation, subsequently declaring Nivedita his spiritual preceptor (ஞானகுரு) and dedicating his first anthology of national songs to her.

4.0 JURISPRUDENTIAL & MODERN RELEVANCE Precursor to Constitutional Gender Equality

Decades before the framing of the Constitution of India, Bharati’s polemics formulated legal paradigms that materialized in Articles 14, 15, and 21. His essays outlined three concrete civil mandates:

I. PROPERTY RIGHTS

Demanded unconditional coparcenary rights for daughters in ancestral property, codified in India only in 2005.

II. UNIVERSAL LITERACY

Universal compulsory schooling regardless of caste or sex, opposing British education policy elitism.

III. MATRIMONIAL CHOICE

Right of adult women to veto arranged child marriages and pursue independent livelihoods.
